Pirutsiagutiit, a new Inuit-led program for Inuit children, will replace the Inuit Child First Initiative in 2027

Call #3

News

On November 18, 2026, Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami President Natan Obed and Indigenous Services Minister Mandy Gull-Masty released the Pirutsiagutiit National Framework. Pirutsiagutiit, meaning "resources for growing well" in Inuktut, will replace the interim Inuit Child First Initiative on April 1, 2027. Budget 2026 proposes $575 million over five years, starting in 2027-28, to renew the initiative and support the transition. Each of the four Inuit Treaty Organizations will co-develop a regional delivery approach with Canada, and supports eligible under the interim initiative will remain eligible.

Context

TRC Call #3 asks all levels of government to fully implement Jordan's Principle so First Nations children get the services they need without delay. The Inuit Child First Initiative was set up in 2018 as an interim Inuit-specific approach. Since then it has helped Inuit children access more than 330,000 health, social and educational products, services and supports, according to the government. Moving to an Inuit-led program is a structural change; it does not by itself show that children are receiving timely services.

Accountability

The funding is proposed in a budget and is not yet approved spending. The Inuvialuit Regional Corporation said a framework is a starting point, not a result. Key questions: will the funding meet demand, how will wait times and outcomes be reported, and how will complaints be handled once Inuit organizations take over delivery?
