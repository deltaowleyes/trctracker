SFU describes Indigenous-led AI project supporting endangered language learning

Call #14Call #15

News

On October 28, 2026, Simon Fraser University reported on First Languages AI Reality (FLAIR), a project led by Indigenous scientists Caroline and Michael Running Wolf. Hosted through SFU's Digital Democracies Institute and Cedar Supercomputing Centre, the initiative is developing language-learning and speech tools with four communities: Gwich'in in the Northwest Territories, Kwakwaka'wakw on Vancouver Island, Makah in Washington State and Yawanawá in Brazil.

Education

SFU says the project begins with community-led language curricula and datasets, and that materials co-developed with communities remain their property. It describes AI learning tools as still under development, not proven replacements for fluent speakers, teachers or community language programs. TRC Calls #14 and #15 concern Indigenous language rights, revitalization and institutional support; this research is relevant context but does not demonstrate fulfillment of either Call.

Accountability

What consent, access controls and Indigenous data-governance agreements guide each pilot? Will communities determine when tools are ready for use, and will independent measures show whether they support language learning? Those are questions for follow-up, not claims of wrongdoing.
