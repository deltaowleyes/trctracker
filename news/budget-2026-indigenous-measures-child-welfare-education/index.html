Budget 2026 proposes long-term funding for Jordan's Principle, child welfare and First Nations education

Call #3Call #4Call #8Call #11

News

Budget 2026 was tabled on November 5, 2026. In a November 13 release, Crown-Indigenous Relations Minister Rebecca Alty highlighted more than $50 billion in proposed Indigenous investments. They include $6 billion over six years for Jordan's Principle, $806.7 million over five years to implement the federal law on First Nations, Inuit and Métis children, youth and families, $5.8 billion over five years for on-reserve elementary and secondary education, and $437.9 million over five years for the Post-Secondary Student Support Program. The release also lists $18.5 billion over 10 years for on-reserve infrastructure and $11 billion over 10 years for First Nations housing.

Context

Several of these lines map onto specific Calls: Jordan's Principle (Call #3), Indigenous child-welfare legislation (Call #4), closing the on-reserve education funding gap (Call #8) and post-secondary funding (Call #11). The release does not mention funding for the search for missing children and burial sites, residential school records, or the National Council for Reconciliation.

Accountability

These are budget proposals, not money spent or results achieved. A funding commitment does not show that a Call has been completed. Watch whether the measures pass, how the money compares with what First Nations organizations say is needed, and whether outcomes such as wait times, graduation rates and the number of children in care are publicly reported.
