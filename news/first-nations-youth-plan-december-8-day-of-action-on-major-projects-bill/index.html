First Nations youth plan a December 8 day of action against the federal major projects bill

Call #43

News

First Nations youth leaders from Attawapiskat First Nation, Ramon Kataquapit of the Okiniwak Youth Movement and Jeronimo Kataquapit of Here We Stand, are planning a national day of action on December 8, 2026, The Canadian Press reported on November 20. That is the day hundreds of chiefs are expected in Ottawa for an Assembly of First Nations meeting. The Canadian Press reports that the Chiefs of Ontario, representing 133 First Nations, support the day of action.

Context

The youth oppose the federal government's proposed major projects legislation, which The Canadian Press describes as a 243-page bill that would fast-track approvals, shorten reviews and create "national interest" zones. TRC Call #43 asks governments to fully adopt and implement the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples as the framework for reconciliation; the Declaration sets standards on free, prior and informed consent. "We have to draw a line in the sand and say, 'No more,'" Jeronimo Kataquapit said.

Accountability

The bill has not passed; The Canadian Press reports it is still before a House of Commons committee. The day of action is planned, not yet held. Ramon Kataquapit says protests will follow across the country if the bill passes. Watch whether the committee changes the bill in response to First Nations concerns, how the government explains its consistency with the UN Declaration Act, and what chiefs decide at the December meeting.
