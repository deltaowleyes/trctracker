How status is assessed

Progress labels are third-party classifications by Indigenous Watchdog, not findings by TRC Tracker. A status label is not proof of implementation: each Call needs evidence of action, evidence of outcomes, Indigenous-led perspectives and dated sources. Government announcements are not proof of completion.

The Government of Canada also publishes federal progress reporting. It covers the 76 Calls involving federal leadership, while Indigenous Watchdog classifies all 94, so their percentages are not directly comparable.

1 Reduce Indigenous children in careChild Welfare Stalled

What is required

  • Monitor and assess neglect investigations.
  • Resource Indigenous communities and child-welfare organizations to keep families together safely.
  • Ensure culturally appropriate environments for children.
  • Train child-welfare decision-makers on residential school history and impacts.
  • Require decision-makers to consider the impacts of residential schools.

Who must answer

Federal, provincial, territorial and Indigenous governments

Independent progress status

Stalled According to Indigenous Watchdog, as of October 1, 2026. Not a TRC Tracker finding.

Why this Call matters

Residential schools separated children from families and communities. Today, child-welfare policy must be judged by family safety, cultural continuity and the outcomes experienced by Indigenous children.

Hard truth

A child-welfare law is not the same as a demonstrated reduction in Indigenous children entering care.
